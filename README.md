# Providentia — Cat Pomodoro Clock

## Vision

A physical desk companion for students and professionals who care about their workspace aesthetic. Not a timer. A personality.

The product lives in the gap between a cheap kitchen utility and a proper lifestyle accessory. It charges a premium not because it does more — but because it *feels* like something.

---

## Market Position

### The competitor

Generic digital timers from Shenzhen factories. 7-segment displays, injection-molded plastic, R$15–30. They own high-volume, low-margin territory. The only way to beat them on their turf is through factory-scale production — that's not our game.

### Why we don't compete there

Using a 7-segment display makes us invisible. The customer sees a R$99 kitchen timer and walks. The aesthetic moat only works if the product *looks* like it belongs to a different category entirely.

### Our position

**Category:** Desk / Lifestyle Accessory — not Utility.

**Target customer:** Students and young professionals building intentional workspaces. The Studygram community. People who buy mechanical keyboards, aesthetic desk mats, and film photos of their coffee while they study.

**What they're buying:** A companion that makes focused work feel cozy. The meow is a reward. The LCD glow is ambiance. The pomodoro method is the excuse.

| | Generic Competitor | Cat Pomodoro |
|---|---|---|
| Display | 7-segment | 16x2 LCD |
| Sound | Industrial beep | Randomized meow |
| Category | Kitchen utility | Desk companion |
| Price | R$15–30 | R$99 |
| Marketing | Price & volume | Aesthetic & personality |

---

## Why R$99 Works

The price is justified by the *category shift*, not the BOM. A customer comparing us to a kitchen timer will not buy. A customer who finds us through a desk setup TikTok will not hesitate.

Gross margin at R$99 with a Nano-based BOM lands around 35–45%. Viable for small-batch and handmade runs.

---

## The Viral Factor

The things that make this shareable aren't the hardware — they're the personality details:

- **The meow.** Randomized pitch and duration. Never identical. Organic. People will film it.
- **Custom LCD icons.** Cat faces, steaming coffee cups, sleep indicators. This is what makes a 16x2 LCD feel designed, not generic.
- **The break indicator.** Work session vs. rest session should feel visually and sonically different. The product should *celebrate* the break.

These details are what someone captions in a reel. They're also what no Shenzhen factory will ever bother to build.

---

## Product Plan

### Must-have before launch

- [ ] Custom LCD character sprites — cat face, coffee cup, sleep/rest icon
- [ ] Visual distinction between work session and break session
- [ ] At least two buttons — mode toggle + start/stop
- [ ] Enclosure design that reads as intentional, not prototyped

### Nice-to-have

- [ ] Session counter (how many pomodoros completed today)
- [ ] Adjustable work/break duration via buttons
- [ ] Idle animation on LCD when not in a session
- [ ] Optional: physical power switch with a satisfying click

### Production path

- Swap Arduino Mega (testing only) → Nano clone for final BOM
- Evaluate custom PCB vs perfboard for first batch (JLCPCB 5-unit run is cheap enough to try early)
- Enclosure: 3D-printed first batch, evaluate injection mold only if demand justifies it

---

## Marketing Strategy

**Channel:** Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels). The product demos itself — put it on a desk, press the button, let the meow happen.

**Content angle:** "I built a pomodoro clock that meows at me and now I actually study." Authentic, slightly absurd, shareable.

**Community:** Studygram, r/battlestations, r/mechanicalkeyboards adjacent audiences. These communities reward aesthetic craft over spec sheets.

**Pricing psychology:** R$99 is a single night out. Positioned correctly, it's an impulse buy for the right customer — not a considered purchase.
